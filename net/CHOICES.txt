Amplifier choices for the parts on hand
=====================================

Build today: lm386n-simple-amp.net
Single +6 V supply, gain 20, 8-ohm speaker, no external transistors,
no volume control. IC plus six external passive components:
  1uF input coupling capacitor
  100nF ceramic and 100uF electrolytic supply decoupling
  250uF output coupling capacitor (220uF or 470uF practical alternatives)
  10-ohm resistor in series with 50nF capacitor from output to ground
    (47nF is a practical standard value)
Gain pins 1 and 8 are open. Pin 7 is open (optional 10uF bypass to ground).
DIP-8: input to pin 3 through coupling capacitor; pins 2 and 4 ground;
pin 6 +6 V; pin 5 output to coupling capacitor and series RC network.
Output capacitor positive lead faces pin 5. Supply capacitor positive
lead faces +6 V. Use short wiring and decouple at the IC.
Start with a 1kHz sine of 20-50mV PEAK. 50mV peak = 100mV peak-to-peak.
Power off before wiring. This version has no volume control: normal
line/headphone output may overdrive it. Start at low source volume.
An actual speaker has reactive impedance; the model uses an 8-ohm resistor.

Existing lm386n-amp.net adds a volume pot and pin-7 bypass capacitor.
The LM386N suffix (-1/-3/-4) must be checked before raising the supply.

For an op-amp plus BD139/BD140 experiment on +/-6 V:
  ne5532-bd139-amp.net  - preferred audio-oriented op-amp choice
  jrc4558-bd139-amp.net - useful second choice if you have more spares
  lm358-bd139-amp.net   - LM358 or LM358P, educational/voice use
  lm324-bd139-amp.net   - similar role, four channels per package
  741-bd139-amp.net     - LM741CN or UA741, educational comparison

All five use gain 11, an 8-ohm load and the same transistor stage.
They add a DC return for the input, replace the zero-ohm driver resistor
with a zero-volt connection, add base stopper resistors and decoupling.
The original extracted decks are preserved. +/-6 V means TWO rails with
12 V between them, not a single +6 V supply.

IMPORTANT MODEL LIMIT: these five decks contain a generic one-pole,
rail-clamped op-amp model with illustrative bandwidth settings. They are
self-contained topology demonstrations, not verified device macromodels.
They omit noise, slew limiting, current limit, input bias/offset, and
internal crossover distortion. No conclusion about actual sound, output
power, thermal safety, or feedback stability follows from these models.
The original simplified transistor models are also unvalidated.
For meaningful comparison replace the approximation with a manufacturer
model and match its subcircuit terminal order. A working schematic still
needs bench validation, especially idle current and high-frequency stability.

Package details:
LM358P is a package variant of LM358, not a higher-performance amplifier.
LM741CN and UA741 are in the 741 family; check vendor/suffix specifications.
NE5532, JRC4558 and LM358 commonly share the dual DIP-8 pin arrangement.
LM324 is DIP-14; 741 is a different DIP-8 pinout. They are not all socket swaps.
Unused op-amp channels must be connected as followers with grounded inputs
on these split supplies; each file documents the physical pin connections.

Other ICs:
TDA2030 is a speaker power amplifier and is the practical choice for more
power with a suitable higher supply and heatsink. It is a different circuit,
not a replacement op-amp in these files. The bare metal tab is tied to pin 3
(negative supply); account for that when mounting a heatsink.
TDA2822 is another low-voltage speaker amplifier option, particularly stereo.
Confirm TDA2822 versus TDA2822M and pin count before creating its wiring:
the 16-pin and 8-pin packages have different pin arrangements.
LM386 is already represented; do not substitute it into an op-amp socket.

Recommendation: LM386 for minimum parts; NE5532 or JRC4558 when exploring
op-amp/transistor stages; TDA2030 for a later higher-power speaker project.
LM358/LM324/741 can demonstrate amplification but are less attractive for
wideband low-distortion audio. Performance depends on the entire circuit,
not an intrinsic ranking of transistor or op-amp 'sound'.
