5 V AUDIO MODULE

2N2222 bench amplifier

Build the left channel on a breadboard, test it with an 8.2 Ω load, then solder the same circuit onto a single-sided PCB. An eight-pin edge connector also fits a wired PAM8403 carrier.

Run two wires per speaker. Connect the left speaker to pins 5 and 7 and the future right speaker to pins 6 and 8. PAM8403 bridge outputs drive both leads. Keep both speaker returns isolated from each other and from pin 3 in the harness. W1 supplies the common return locally on the mono board. A three-wire speaker harness with a shared return requires rewiring before fitting the PAM board.

The PAM column defines how to wire a carrier to a PAM8403 module. Check the specific module’s labels before wiring it. The edge header is a harness interface shared by both boards.

Volume control on the module

RV1 is a 20 kΩ audio-taper pot between the left input terminal and C1. On the breadboard, a 10 kΩ or 20 kΩ audio-taper pot works. Use three short leads to the holes shown. The PCB fits the Bourns PTV09A-1020F-A203, with the shaft pointing up from the board, 2.5 mm signal-pin spacing, and two mounting tabs.

For the gain test, set the pot fully clockwise and measure the input at c8. A future stereo version can use a dual-gang 10 kΩ or 20 kΩ audio pot, with the second gang wired from RIN to the right amplifier input. The dual-gang part needs its own footprint. 1. Breadboard the left channel

  1. Keep power off. Orient the board as shown, with letters a–e above the gap and f–j below it. Holes with the same number and on the same side of the center gap share a strip. Check the two rails for continuity and bridge any split in each rail you use.
  2. Fit U1 across the gap with its notch facing column 1. Place Q1 and Q2 by their pin numbers in the table. Install the resistors, D1/D2 and the capacitors. For a short-leaded 1 µF ceramic C1, use b8 and b6 (5.08 mm apart). Either direction works. Connect the pot wiper to c8 on the b8 strip. The a6–a14 jumper carries C1’s output to U1 pin 3. Match the diode stripe to K. Match each electrolytic lead to the + or − marker. C2 goes + to j22 (+5 V) and − to j24 (ground).
  3. Install the W1 link and every row in the jumper table. Rail positions are counted from the left in the drawing. If your rail-hole pattern differs, use any free hole on the specified rail.
  4. Connect the harness or clip leads to the individual J1 terminals shown in the table. For the first test, connect 5 V at e3, ground at e1, left input at a2, and the dummy load between j1 and j5. With power still off, verify continuity from pin 7 to pin 3 and check there’s no persistent short across pins 4 and 3. Align the DIP notch and recheck the diode bands and C6 polarity.

2. Power up and test

  1. Leave the input and speaker disconnected. Set the bench supply to 5.0 V with a 50 mA limit and connect it between J1 pins 4 (+) and 3 (−). Brief charging current is normal. Switch off if the supply stays in current limit or the idle current settles above 15 mA.
  2. Measure to J1 pin 3. U1 pin 8 should be near 5 V. U1 pins 3, 5 and 7 and C6 positive should settle near 2.5 V. After about 30 seconds with the output open, aim for less than 50 mV DC between pins 5 and 7.
  3. Power off and fit the 8.2 Ω dummy load between pins 5 and 7. Ground the generator and scope to J1 pin 3 on this mono prototype. Turn RV1 fully clockwise, feed a 1 kHz sine into pin 1, and adjust the generator until you measure about 25 mV peak-to-peak at the wiper (c8). Use the generator’s Hi-Z setting if available.
  4. Power up. Expect about 0.275 Vpp across the load for 25 mVpp in. Inspect the sine for clipping, a notch through zero, or high-frequency oscillation. The LM358P has limited swing toward +5 V, so watch the positive peak as you increase the input. If it’s clean, raise the supply limit to 100 mA and increase the input gradually toward 90 mVpp and stop at the first sign of distortion or at 1 Vpp out (about 15 mW into 8.2 Ω).
  5. Power off, swap the load for the 8 Ω left speaker, and restart at low signal level. Keep the right input and outputs unused on this build. When testing a PAM carrier, use a differential probe across a speaker pair if measuring the output. A grounded scope clip on a PAM speaker terminal can short its output.

The generic op-amp SPICE model gives 2.50 V before C6, about 1.6 mA idle supply current, and 0.275 Vpp into 8.2 Ω at 1 kHz. Measure bias, offset, clipping and stability on the LM358P breadboard. Download SPICE deck

3. Solder the PCB after the bench test

All tracks and solder pads are on the bottom copper. Through-hole components plus W1 and W2 go on the top. W1 connects the mono left speaker return to ground.
